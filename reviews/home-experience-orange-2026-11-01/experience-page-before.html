How its Made

  1. Picking

    Ripe cocoa pods are cut by hand. Only ready pods are chosen. The tree is protected.

  2. Harversting

    Pods are gathered and carried from the field. Freshness matters. Flavor starts now.

  3. Fermentation

    Beans are removed and left to ferment. Heat builds. Flavor develops.

  4. Drying

    Beans dry in the sun. Moisture fades. The flavor settles.

  5. Roasting

    Heat unlocks cocoa flavor.Aromas deepen. Character forms.

  6. Griding and Coaching

    Beans become chocolate. Time creates smooth texture. Flavor finds balance.

  7. Tempering

    Chocolate is heated and cooled. It gains shine and snap. Ready to enjoy.

Museum & Virtual Exhibition A rich history of chocolate

Every chocolate story begins with cacao. Explore the people, places and discoveries that shaped its journey.

  1. 1500 BCE–900 CE Mesoamerica Ancient Origins

    Olmec and Maya peoples cultivated cacao in Mesoamerica. For the Maya, cacao was a sacred gift from the gods. Used in rituals and ceremonies, cacao drinks became symbols of life, power and connection, long before chocolate took the familiar form of bars and confections.

    Explore the history

  2. 1428–1521 CE Central Mexico The Aztec Empire

    The Aztecs considered cacao a sacred and valuable substance. Cacao drinks were associated with rituals, warriors and special occasions, while cacao beans also served as a form of currency.

    Explore cacao as currency

  3. 1500s From the Americas to Europe Chocolate Comes to Europe

    Spanish travellers brought cacao to Europe in the 1500s. Over time, drinking chocolate became a rare and fashionable luxury among royalty and the elite. Adapted with sugar and spices, it introduced European drinkers to a taste already deeply rooted in Mesoamerican life.

    Explore chocolate in Europe

  4. 1600s–1700s European courts and chocolate houses A Taste Spreads

    Chocolate houses opened across Europe, and drinking chocolate gained popularity in royal courts and aristocratic circles. Expanding trade carried cacao to more people and places, while chocolate pots, porcelain cups and new recipes made enjoying the drink an art of its own.

    Explore the chocolate kitchens

  5. 1800s Factories and new technology The Industrial Revolution

    New machines, improved processing and larger factories transformed chocolate production. Alongside drinking chocolate, solid bars and other confections reached a wider audience. These nineteenth-century advances made chocolate more accessible and helped establish the modern chocolate industry, changing how cacao was processed and enjoyed.

    Explore chocolate-making innovations

  6. 1900s New recipes, familiar names Innovation and Iconic Brands

    Advances in processing, new recipes and familiar brands brought chocolate to more households. Cadbury, Hershey and Nestlé helped shape a growing market for bars and confections, while advertising and new packaging made chocolate increasingly recognizable in shops and homes.

    Explore a chocolate brand timeline

  7. Mid 1900s Chocolate around the world A Global Obsession

    By the mid-1900s, chocolate was part of everyday life and celebration in many countries. Expanding distribution brought familiar brands to new markets, while local tastes shaped different flavors and confections. Chocolate’s global story grew through both large manufacturers and the traditions of local makers.

    Explore chocolate’s global growth

  8. Late 1900s Origin, flavor and craftsmanship Craft and Fine Chocolate

    A renewed focus on quality, cacao origin and craftsmanship brought fresh attention to fine chocolate. Single-origin bars and the growing bean-to-bar movement encouraged makers to explore distinctive flavors. Artisan chocolatiers continued to push the possibilities of technique, texture and design, treating chocolate as both food and creative material.

    Explore fine chocolate and craft

  9. 2000s Cacao communities and ecosystems Sustainability and Impact

    Growing attention to ethical sourcing, farmer livelihoods and environmental stewardship has reshaped chocolate’s story. Certification, traceability and direct trading relationships aim to improve accountability across the cocoa supply chain. Supporting cacao-growing communities and protecting tropical ecosystems remain essential challenges for the industry.

    Explore cocoa sustainability

  10. Today & Tomorrow Creativity and the future of chocolate A Sweeter Tomorrow

    Chocolate continues to evolve through new flavors, ingredient choices and creative applications. Sustainability, fairness and traceability remain central challenges for its future. From everyday bars to sculpted showpieces, chocolate offers room for craft, imagination and shared enjoyment in a more connected world.

    Explore cocoa traceability
